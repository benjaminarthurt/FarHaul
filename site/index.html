Build your ship. Haul the freight. Push the frontier.

Far Haul is a first-person freight, ship-building and exploration game. You own a small, secondhand ship, a modest bank balance and a strong opinion about fuel prices. You take contracts and move goods between worlds, because someone has to. This page shows what you can play today and what is still on the developer's list.

The game, and where it is today

Far Haul is about owning a working spaceship. The ship is a place you walk through, and freight is how you pay for it. The bank would like you to remember the second part.

You start as an independent operator with a cheap ship and just enough money to keep it flying, if nothing breaks. You take contracts, load containers, work out whether a job is worth the fuel, and run goods between established worlds. Over time you build bigger, buy range, and head out past the end of the charts, where there's no repair yard, no fuel depot and nobody to call. The farther you go, the less there is to help you, which is either the appeal or the warning.

What you can play today: design a ship from modules and find out whether it works, take freight from a simulated economy of eighteen systems, and fly it. You start with a sublight ship, so your first jobs are runs between the port, fuel depot, moon and belt of your own system, flown by hand or left to the autopilot. Save up for an FTL drive and the other systems open up, with a jump that takes you there without a cut. You can also get up from the helm and walk the ship while it flies. When you are berthed you are on your feet in the port, walking between the freight office, the fuel desk, the shipyard and the bar, where people ask for rush jobs, passage and sealed crates. Fit lander legs and every moon opens up: a base and a mining camp to land at, missions to do on foot in a suit, and an outpost, an ice mine and a deep crater to fly out to. And now there are space walks: out of the airlock on a tether with a wrench and a torch, patching the hull yourself while the ship coasts, unless somebody left the engine on. Most days a derelict drifts near the station, with salvage panels to torch off its hull and a cargo hatch to cut open: crates, supplies and ship parts to haul home and sell. Other ships come and go now too: haulers, station pods, survey craft and ships in orbit, and out on the long runs, now and then, a ship whose jump drive gave up halfway. Or hire a crew at the bar to do the repairs for you: engineers, deck techs, pilots, loadmasters and stewards, each with a name, a wage and a job that makes the ship better at its own. You can fire them too, for two days' pay. And the whole economy now opens its books: press N for the trade report, with where the freight is going, who's running short, who's earning, and the dent you're making in it.

Around the game

Help

You start with a ready-made sublight hauler and 110,000 to 220,000 credits, depending on difficulty. Take local freight, save for an FTL drive, then go further. The steps below are plain on purpose: some things shouldn't be funny.

Your first ship

  1. Download and unzip the game, run FarHaul.exe, then choose New game. Pick a name, race, difficulty, starting world and save slot, then Begin. Later, use Continue or Load game to return.
  2. You arrive on foot in the port's concourse. Walk up to a desk and press E to use it. The Shipyard desk opens the ship builder. T opens the station terminal, which has every action as a menu.
  3. Look at the ship. Click a part in the palette on the left, then click next to the ship to place it. Green means it fits. Red tells you why not.
  4. Watch the power, cooling and cargo meters and the warnings on the right. Each warning says what is missing.
  5. Use Cargo preview to fill the hold and see the ship loaded. This only changes how the ship looks and weighs. It does not buy or sell anything.
  6. Type a name in Ship name. Press S to save. Your game also autosaves. Leave shipyard (L) saves and takes you back to the concourse.
  7. At the Freight office, take a run and load it. A sublight ship sees runs inside its own system. Choose Depart on autopilot to have it flown at the textbook fuel cost, or Fly the run yourself. The people at the bar have other work: rush jobs, passengers and sealed crates.
  8. Fit lander legs to land on a moon. At the base's hab, the suit store sells kit and dispatch posts missions; the airlock desk suits you up and puts you outside.
  9. Fit braking thrusters on the side of the hull, nozzles to the bow, and hold B to slow down without turning round. The braking autopilot uses them too, which makes docking much less of a pirouette.
  10. Add manoeuvring thrusters and hold Ctrl with the arrow keys to slide sideways or up and down without turning. Together with braking thrusters, the autopilot can line you up and stop you nose-on.
  11. Away from a berth, the airlock takes you on a space walk. Buy patch kits at the repair desk, then fix the scorch marks: 1 wrench for loose plating, 2 torch and a kit for holes, hold E. Up to 50%; past that it's yard work. Watch the air gauge, and don't leave the engine running.
  12. Fit crew bunks, then hire hands at the bar: a day's pay to sign them on, their wage every day after. An engineer, a deck tech and a pilot take most of the chores off you. Click a crew member twice to let them go.
  13. Fit an FTL drive in the shipyard (about 48,000 cr with its radiator) to take freight to other systems. At the freight office or the gate, Fly the jump undocks you and lets you press J once you are clear.

What makes a ship valid

  • A cockpit to fly from.
  • At least one engine pointing aft.
  • A sealed hull. Cyan rings mark doorways that open onto space. Join another room to one, or bolt a door cap over it: 250 cr plain, 600 cr with a porthole. Turn the cap to face the doorway. Hatch caps close a ladder shaft's top or bottom, and parts can still bolt on over any cap.
  • An airlock, so the crew can get in and out.
  • Enough power for everything that draws it.
  • Enough cooling for the heat you make.
  • Enough thrust for the loaded weight. The stats panel shows thrust-to-weight empty and loaded.
  • An FTL drive, if the freight crosses between systems.

Questions

How do I use the ship remote?

Turn on Ship remote in Settings. It shows an address and a six-digit code: open the address in the browser on a phone or tablet on the same Wi-Fi and enter the code. Windows will ask once to let Far Haul through the firewall; allow it on private networks. The remote does what the keys do, with the same limits, so it can't fly you past the game's own rules. In port it does what the desks do; flying a run yourself still starts in the game. New code unpairs every device.

Can I cheat?

For testing, yes. Turn on Debug cheats in Settings and the pause menu (Esc) gets two more buttons: a million credits, and a full repair and resupply (in flight it fills the tanks too). The save remembers that you used them. It won't judge. Much.

Where is my game saved?

In a Far Haul saves folder next to FarHaul.exe, automatically, every time you change something. The game is more careful with your progress than the bank is. Copy that folder with the exe to take your games to another computer. If the exe sits somewhere it cannot write (such as Program Files), saves go to your AppData folder instead; the Load game screen shows where.

I cannot place a part. Why?

Move the cursor over the ship and read the red message under it. The usual reasons are overlapping another module, no matching door or mount on the neighbouring part, or not enough credits. It's usually the credits.

Why can I only see a few jobs?

Without an FTL drive your ship only sees runs inside its own system, and only ones its tank can lift: the hold is limited by fuel, so a long hop means a lighter load. Fit an FTL drive and the interstellar board opens up. The people at the bar have more work than the board shows, and lander legs open surface runs that pay more. Ambition, as ever, costs fuel.

There is no sound.

Check the volume sliders in Settings on the title screen, and press M in the shipyard to check sound isn't muted there. Space is silent; the game isn't supposed to be.

It runs slowly.

Open Settings from the title screen and set Graphics quality to Low: it turns off shadows and anti-aliasing. If the game will not start at all, your graphics drivers may be too old for Vulkan; the game then falls back to a simpler renderer and soldiers on.

Does it run on Mac, Linux, a phone or in a browser?

Not at the moment. Far Haul is built for Windows PCs with a mouse and keyboard. The early browser version has been retired, with honours.

I ran out of air outside.

The base or camp crew drag you back aboard and bill you 800 cr, and any mission you held fails. Nobody mentions it again, except on the invoice. Watch the AIR readout top left: running uses air half as fast again, and it warns you below 20%. Bigger tanks at a hab's suit store give you up to 35 minutes.

How do I get to another star?

Fit an FTL drive in the shipyard, accept an interstellar contract, and choose Fly the jump at the freight office. Get 3 km clear of the station, slow down and let the engines cool, then press J. A jump costs fuel, fees and days of crew wages, and the world keeps moving while you travel. Prices too, usually upward.

About Far Haul

A game about owning a spacecraft and being free to go somewhere. Free in the philosophical sense; the fuel isn't.

Not a magical ship, and not a legendary warship. A machine you designed, paid for, patched together, overloaded and eventually know well enough to hear when something is wrong. There's no chosen hero and no galaxy to save. There's always another shipment, another settlement, and another star past the edge of the chart, plus an invoice for getting there.

What it is built around

  • Function over formShips are boxes, frames, tanks, engines and radiators. A strange-looking ship that obeys the engineering is a perfectly good ship. Beauty is optional; radiators aren't.
  • The ship is the placeEvery compartment is a real room. Cockpit, hold and engine room all exist, and walking between them takes time. Long ships, long walks.
  • Freight is the economyCivilisation needs things moved. You earn by moving them, and what you earn becomes a better ship, eventually, after the bank.
  • The frontier gets thinPast the core worlds there is less help. Eventually there's no repair yard, no fuel depot, and no rescue. Pack accordingly.

Known space

Humans, Kesh, Ilyan, Vey and Orun each have a home system and an engineering tradition. Trade has mixed them for generations, so a Kesh captain at a human-built terminal is ordinary. Your species shapes where you come from, not what you can do. The core is crowded and well supplied. Further out there are fewer ports, fewer repairs and less rescue, until Lastlight, where ordinary commerce runs out.

The prototype

Version
0.11.0, prototype
Built with
Godot 4.4, written in GDScript, using the Forward+ renderer (Vulkan) on Windows.
Art and sound
The textures, sky, logo and every sound effect (engines, footsteps, room tone, the suit, the jump) are generated in code. Earth's coastlines come from NOAA's public-domain GLOBE elevation data. The theme song is a custom track. The intro video is a placeholder, and knows it.
Placeholders
Prices, part stats and the intro video will all change, usually at the worst possible moment.
