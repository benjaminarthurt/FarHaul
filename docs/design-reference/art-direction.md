# Far Haul — Art & Design Reference

## Primary Direction

**Commercial/industrial spaceflight, not military sci-fi.**

The target blend is closer to:

- heavy construction equipment
- container ports and cargo terminals
- locomotives
- research vessels
- submarines
- spacecraft engineering
- offshore industrial platforms

than to sleek fighters, luxury yachts, or naval warships.

The world should feel like **heavy industry discovered interstellar travel**.

## Core Visual Rule

> **Nothing exists merely because it looks futuristic. Every object should look like somebody designed it to perform a job, somebody manufactured it at a price, and eventually somebody will have to repair it.**

---

## 1. Ship Exteriors

### Desired qualities

- boxy and modular
- visible structural frames
- external tanks
- external cargo racks
- radiator panels
- maneuvering thruster pods
- antennas and communications hardware
- obvious docking hardware
- service panels and access points
- huge engines where appropriate
- large, mechanically believable landing gear
- asymmetry is acceptable
- mismatched modules and paint are acceptable
- space-only vessels do not need aerodynamic hulls

### Avoid

- fighter-aircraft silhouettes
- ornamental wings
- unnecessary pointed noses
- smooth monolithic hulls with no visible service access
- designs where every system is hidden beneath sculpted bodywork
- military styling as the default visual language

A freighter should read visually as:

**cockpit + habitation + warehouse + power plant + engines**

rather than as a giant airplane.

---

## 2. Real-World Heavy-Equipment Inspiration

Use construction equipment, mining machinery, locomotives, cranes, and industrial vehicles for scale and detailing.

Useful visual cues:

- welded structural members
- hydraulic cylinders
- bolted service panels
- lifting points
- safety rails
- serial-number plates
- warning labels
- replaceable equipment modules
- oversized hinges
- exposed plumbing
- rugged access ladders
- visible fasteners

Landing gear should look like machinery that supports thousands of tonnes, not a small decorative appendage disappearing into a hull.

---

## 3. Cargo Operations

Container terminals are a major reference.

Cargo operations should include:

- standardized freight modules
- cranes
- straddle carriers
- forklifts
- orbital handling frames
- cargo tractors
- tie-down systems
- markings and destination codes
- oversized industrial loads

Example cargo labeling:

```text
FHCU 882193-4
12 SCU
MASS 38,440 KG

PORT NEW HOUSTON
DEST: KEPLER 62 / HESPERUS

PRESSURIZED
DO NOT VENT
```

Experienced players should be able to recognize categories of cargo visually.

---

## 4. Starter Ships

The starter vessel should resemble a **space box truck** more than a miniature starship.

Suggested structure:

1. cockpit / cab
2. tiny habitation compartment
3. cargo box
4. engineering space
5. engines

It should immediately communicate that the player owns a commercial working vehicle.

Early vessels should be cramped, inexpensive, somewhat awkward, and visibly serviceable.

---

## 5. Interiors

Primary real-world references:

- submarines
- ISS modules
- ship engine rooms
- research vessels
- locomotive equipment spaces
- industrial mechanical rooms

Small ships should be dense.

Nearly every wall should perform a function.

Possible details:

- storage above bunks
- electrical cabinet beside the lavatory
- oxygen bottles attached to bulkheads
- folding furniture
- exposed pipes overhead
- removable floor panels
- equipment racks
- emergency signage
- physical labels added by previous owners

**Empty space is luxury.**

Larger ships can become more comfortable, but additional volume should primarily exist because the vessel has more cargo, machinery, crew, or mission capability.

---

## 6. Cockpits and Bridges

Reference commercial aircraft, locomotives, cranes, and real spacecraft rather than fictional fighter cockpits.

Use a combination of:

- physical switches
- emergency controls
- displays
- overhead electrical panels
- navigation screens
- engine controls
- communications hardware
- circuit breakers
- manufacturer-specific interfaces

Do not make every control a floating hologram or touchscreen.

Physical emergency controls improve readability and reinforce the machine-like nature of the ship.

---

## 7. Engineering Spaces

Engineering areas should resemble real mechanical infrastructure.

Reference:

- pump rooms
- ship engine rooms
- power plants
- industrial mechanical rooms
- process piping systems

A reactor should not simply be a glowing object in an empty room.

It should exist within a larger support system containing:

- pumps
- coolant lines
- heat exchangers
- electrical distribution
- pressure systems
- valves
- control cabinets
- instrumentation
- emergency isolation equipment

Players should eventually learn machinery by sight.

---

## 8. EVA and Exterior Maintenance

The ship's exterior must be designed for human maintenance.

Include:

- handrails
- tether points
- access panels
- external service connectors
- maintenance paths
- identification labels
- replaceable equipment
- structural frames usable as handholds

During EVA, the player should be able to identify the internal layout from the exterior.

The player should be able to think:

> My cabin is behind that panel. Cargo is underneath. The reactor compartment starts there.

The exterior and interior should feel like two views of the same physical object.

---

## 9. Frontier Landing Sites

Frontier settlements should often be small and practical.

Possible visual elements:

- concrete or prepared landing pads
- fuel tanks
- warehouses
- communications towers
- pressure buildings
- prefab structures
- cargo containers repurposed as buildings
- surface vehicles
- cranes
- dirt and mud
- improvised additions
- small commercial areas

Avoid enormous futuristic cities as the default.

A settlement of a few hundred or few thousand people should visually resemble an industrial outpost that is gradually becoming a town.

---

## 10. Established Ports

Established systems provide contrast through scale and organization.

References should lean toward major seaports and logistics hubs.

Include:

- traffic-control infrastructure
- docking structures
- fuel transfer systems
- maintenance yards
- container storage
- ship dealerships
- repair docks
- warehouses
- passenger terminals
- dense commercial traffic

The desired feel is closer to **a major container port in orbit** than a fantasy space palace.

---

## 11. Unknown and Uninhabited Worlds

Keep most planets grounded.

Common worlds can be:

- barren
- rocky
- icy
- dusty
- volcanic
- heavily cratered
- chemically hostile
- atmospherically extreme

Not every planet should be visually spectacular.

Restraint makes exceptional discoveries matter.

Occasional major visual moments can include:

- enormous gas giants filling the sky
- ring systems
- ice cliffs
- oceans
- severe weather
- unusual geology
- genuinely living environments

---

## 12. Material Language

Favor:

- painted metals
- anodized metals
- composites
- thermal insulation
- rubberized seals
- industrial plastics
- exposed fasteners
- weathered surfaces
- replaced panels
- patched paint
- faded warning markings

Wear should reflect use.

Cargo ships acquire scrapes near loading areas.

Landing vessels show abrasion and dust.

External hardware shows radiation and thermal exposure.

Frequently opened service panels have worn fasteners and hand-contact marks.

---

## 13. Manufacturer Identity

Manufacturers should have recognizable engineering philosophies rather than simply different skins.

Examples:

- cheap, standardized, easy-to-repair modules
- massive industrial equipment known for durability
- lightweight exploration hardware
- old military-surplus equipment that is rugged but inefficient
- premium equipment that is compact and quiet but expensive and harder to service

A player should eventually recognize a component family visually before reading its name.
