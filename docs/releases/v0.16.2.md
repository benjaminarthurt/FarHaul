Far Haul 0.16.2 for Windows, in which everyone on the radio finally says what the subtitles have been saying all along, and some of them sound like they mean it.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.16.2.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they don't talk. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

### Voices
- Traffic control, pad control, the foreman, haulers, the patrol, the port tug, rival salvage crews and anyone sending a mayday now speak their lines out loud. 2,369 recordings, 58 voices.
- Every number they'd say is recorded too: your berth, the fine, the tonnes on offer, how many are aboard the ship in trouble. Names they don't say: the subtitle still has those, and nobody wants to hear your ship's name mispronounced.
- Each species talks like itself, not just in its own pitch. Kesh are formal and heavy with duty ("The collar has been inspected and signed off"). Ilyan are clipped and exact, and resent wasted words. Vey are warm and think about everyone downstream. Orun say "we", always, and assume everyone is listening, because everyone is.
- And sounds like itself. Each call opens in the caller's own language until the translator catches up with a chirp; the second time, it catches up quicker. Kesh voices carry a second voice an octave down, Ilyan a faint glassy one up, Vey ripple like they're talking through water, and Orun sound like several of them at once, in a tunnel. A station keeps the same controller every time you call. So does a ship.
- It all comes through a bad radio: squelch, hiss that gets worse with distance, the roger beep. Clean enough to follow, dirty enough to be a radio.
- Passengers say their piece out loud when you walk up to them, in their own words. Mostly about the bed. An Orun in a single stateroom feels lonely.
- Settings has a **Recorded voices** switch. Off brings back the old babble, for purists and the nostalgic.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, at a dignified pace.
- The download is about 90 MB bigger. Voices are heavy. So is freight.
- Your own lines are still typed, not spoken. You know what you said.
