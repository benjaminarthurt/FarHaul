Far Haul 0.15.0 for Windows, in which you can carry more freight, and the passengers stop sleeping in the engineer's bunk.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.15.0.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they travel steerage. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

### Bigger holds
- **Cargo hold 2x2**: 8 containers, 52 t, 11,000 cr. **Cargo bay 2x3**: 12 containers, 84 t, 15,000 cr. Both are cheaper and lighter per tonne than stacking the little 1x2 hold, and you can walk in both: containers two high along the walls, an open floor down the middle, and a gantry that has seen things.
- The catch is the footprint. Eighty tonnes in one place is hard to keep near the centre of mass, and the builder will mention it.

### Passengers, riding along
- Passengers are no longer a job that takes over the whole trip. Take your freight contract, then fill the beds as well. Each party gets off where it's going, pays, and leaves the bunk smelling faintly of someone else.
- **Local passengers** still ask in person at the bar, the hab's dispatch desk and the camp foreman's. **Long-haul passengers** now ask at the freight office, for passage to other systems your ship can reach. They want a working FTL drive. Most people do.
- They pay on arrival. A hard landing on the way halves the fare. So does showing up late: local parties expect to arrive within a couple of days, long-haul ones by the due day on their booking. They notice.
- No contract on? The freight office will fly them there for you on autopilot, or let you fly a local hop by hand.

### Cabins
- Three new rooms, apart from the crew's bunks:
  - **Passenger cabin**: 1x1, 2 beds, 7,000 cr. 1.3× the fare. The door locks.
  - **Passenger quarters 2x2**: 4 beds in curtained pods around a lounge with a sofa and a screen, 16,000 cr. 1.6× the fare.
  - **Stateroom 2x2**: 2 beds, a real double bed, armchairs, a long viewport and a plant that somebody waters. 26,000 cr. 2.5× the fare, for travellers who don't ask the price.
- A spare crew bunk still takes a passenger at the basic fare. Each party gets the best free beds and pays for the beds it gets, so a stateroom earns its keep even on people who'd have slept in a cupboard.
- Some passengers insist on a cabin, or the stateroom, and won't take a bunk. The board says who wants what, and what they'd pay in the beds you've got free.

### Aboard
- Passengers are aboard when you walk the ship, in the rooms they paid for. They dress for travel, not work: long coats in colours no crew job uses, bright scarves, a hat now and then, and a bag they won't put down. Walk up and they'll tell you where they're going and what they think of the bed.
- A steward now looks after the passenger rooms first, and still earns better fares.

### Elsewhere
- Rescued survivors take a spare crew bunk first, then whatever bed is free. Nobody gets left behind for want of a pillow.
- Saves from 0.14 with a passenger run on carry on: the passengers are now riding along.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, at a stately pace.
- Long-haul passengers only ride the autopilot, or a jump you fly with a contract. There's no hand-flown jump with only passengers aboard yet.
- Passengers stand rather than sit. They paid for chairs. They're not using them.
