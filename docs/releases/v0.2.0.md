Far Haul 0.2.0 for Windows, in which the ship finally gets dressed before leaving the house.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.2.0.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they contain exactly zero spaceships. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. It's still a freighter game. It now just looks like one.

### A hull, at last
- Until now you flew a stack of open-topped rooms through hard vacuum, and the crew were very polite about it. In flight, on the title screen, in the dock and in the port windows, the compartments now sit inside a plated outer hull.
- Edges and corners are rounded, so it looks less like a shipping container that took a wrong turn. It's still mostly a shipping container. We're realists.
- The hull leaves the useful holes open: the cockpit windscreen and side windows, the airlock's outer hatch and the reactor vents. Doorways you never built onto are plated over, which is what doorways onto space should always have been.
- Engines, radiators, tanks, the jump drive and cargo racks now sit outside the hull where they belong. Landing legs stay put, because legs are legs.
- The shipyard still shows the ship without its hull, so you can see what you're paying for.

### The engine stops pretending
- The engine was a cone with a lid on it. Rocket science has opinions about lids. It's now an open bell with a copper rim and a throat glowing deep inside.
- The flame was an orange traffic cone. Now it's a pale, hot core with faint shock bands, inside a softer blue glow that spreads and fades as it leaves the bell.
- It grows longer and brighter as you open the throttle and shrinks as you ease off, so you can watch your fuel bill happen.
- Seen from the chase camera, straight up the exhaust, it glows at the mouth instead of vanishing politely.
- Lander lift jets get small bells and flames of their own.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, wheezing slightly.
- Prototype numbers: prices, pay and tuning will change, usually at the worst possible moment.
- The hull is the same plating on every ship from every yard. Manufacturers with taste are coming. Taste is expensive.
- No space walks, planets with air or crew yet. Now that there's an outside to the ship, someone will eventually have to go and stand on it.
