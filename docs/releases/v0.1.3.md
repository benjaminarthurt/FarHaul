Far Haul 0.1.3 for Windows: course hold, a tug that finishes the run, and time compression that slows down near the target instead of stopping.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.1.3.zip` under Assets below (not the Source code files), unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

### Course hold (V)
- Point the nose within 5° of the target's diamond and press **V**. Course hold then keeps your direction of travel on the target.
- It steers, and you keep the throttle. While you burn, it takes out sideways drift first, then pushes you toward the target. While you coast, it trims drift with small burns and rests the nose on the target.
- A turn key, **X** (the brake autopilot takes over) or **V** again switches it off.
- If you're more than 5° off, **V** tells you how far off you are.

### The tug takes you to the nearer end
- Out of fuel on a run, **Esc** calls a tug to whichever is nearer. If that's the destination, the run is delivered. Otherwise it tows you back to the start.
- A tug can't land you, so runs to a moon pad or camp are always towed back.
- The prompt says which way the tug will go and what it costs. The arrival message lists the berth fee and the tow separately.

### Time compression
- It now starts 1 km out from the station, instead of 3 km.
- Near the target it slows down instead of stopping: x5 within 25 km and x2 within 5 km. It also keeps the target at least 10 real seconds away at your current speed.
- Your chosen rate comes back by itself once you're clear. While it's capped, the readout shows the rate running, the rate you set, and why.
- It still runs at x1 on the approach to the station, and at the brake point when you're flying by hand.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation.
- Prototype numbers: prices, pay and tuning will change.
- No space walks, planets with air or crew yet.
