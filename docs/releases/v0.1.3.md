Far Haul 0.1.3 for Windows, in which the ship learns to steer itself, the tug learns to count, and time compression stops slamming on the brakes the moment anything interesting happens.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.1.3.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they contain exactly zero spaceships. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. It's a freighter game. The only thing it steals is your evening.

### Course hold (V): cruise control for people who own a ship
- Point the nose within 5° of the target's diamond and press **V**. The ship keeps your direction of travel on the target from then on. You keep the throttle, because it's still your fuel bill.
- Burning, it takes out sideways drift first and spends the rest getting you there. Coasting, it nudges the drift out with small trim burns and rests the nose on the target, like a dog that knows where dinner is.
- Miss the 5° and **V** tells you how far off you are. Precisely. To the degree. It isn't judging. Much.
- A turn key, **X** (the brake autopilot takes over) or **V** again switches it off.

### The tug can now count to two
- Out of fuel on a run, **Esc** calls a tug to whichever is nearer. Run dry 20 km from the destination and it tows you *in*, and the run is delivered. Previously it dragged you all the way home on principle.
- A tug can't land, so runs to a moon pad or camp still get towed back. It's a tug, not a miracle.
- It's not free. The prompt shows the fee before you commit (900 cr on Normal), and the arrival message lists the berth fee and the tow separately, so you know exactly who to resent.

### Time compression, now with less whiplash
- It starts 1 km out from the station instead of 3 km. The station hasn't got any less interesting. We just stopped making you stare at it.
- Near the target it slows down instead of stopping dead: x5 within 25 km, x2 within 5 km. It also keeps the target at least 10 real seconds away, so you can't sail past it at x60 and wonder where it went.
- Your chosen rate comes back by itself once you're clear. While it's capped, the readout says what it's running at, what you asked for, and why you can't have it.
- It's still x1 on the approach to the station, and at the brake point when you're flying by hand. Some moments deserve to be lived in real time, mostly the ones that cost money.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, wheezing slightly.
- Prototype numbers: prices, pay and tuning will change, usually at the worst possible moment.
- No space walks, planets with air or crew yet. It's quiet out here. Suspiciously quiet.
