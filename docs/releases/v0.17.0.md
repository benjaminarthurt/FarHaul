Far Haul 0.17.0 for Windows, in which the frontier finally notices who's been feeding it, and who hasn't.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.17.0.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they don't grow. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load: everywhere starts at its usual size the day you load them.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

### Frontier growth
- Every port, fuel depot, moon base, belt works, pad and mining camp in known space now has a size, and keeps it only as long as somebody keeps it supplied. Each day it needs so many tonnes brought in. Get them there and it grows. Don't, and it shrinks.
- Somebody usually does: the other carriers. How well they manage follows the real economy. A system whose shelves keep running dry doesn't have much to spare for its moon base.
- You count too, in full. Every tonne you deliver to a site is supply on top of theirs, and every tonne you carry away is trade. Small places need little, so a captain who hauls to Luna and nowhere else will have it a size bigger in a fortnight.
- Nothing caps it. A site can keep growing past the biggest concourse we know how to draw.
- Or it can empty. A place nobody supplies for long enough is **abandoned**: no freight, nobody at the desks, a lamp in three on the emergency circuit. The fuel pump still works, at double the price, because nobody's there to argue with it. What's left can be stripped for salvage every few weeks. If its system booms, someone may move back in.
- Growth sticks, slowly. The haulers learn to keep a grown site fed, and learn to stop coming to one that's fading.
- Systems follow their sites, slower. A bigger system's works make more and its exchange needs more, so it posts more freight. If the haulers can't keep up, it stalls.

### What size gets you
- **Extreme frontier:** freight, fuel and repairs, at 10% over the usual price.
- **Frontier:** passengers.
- **Outer:** crew for hire.
- **Developed and core:** fuel and repairs 5% and 10% cheaper, a bigger concourse with a crowd and planters, more buildings outside the moon base windows.
- **Core**, for a depot, moon station or belt works: its own shipyard. Main ports keep theirs as long as anyone lives there.
- And the reverse, all the way down.

### Seeing it
- **Trade report, page 5 (Growth).** Every site in the system you're in, how big it is against where it started, which way it's going, and why: "short: traffic brings 78% of the 5 t a day it needs", or "you bring 67% of it". Then what grew, shrank or emptied anywhere lately, and every system's size.
- **The place itself.** Concourses grow and shrink with their sites.
- **The radio.** Ask a passing ship for news and it'll tell you what's growing and what's dying, in its own species' words. The Orun take it personally.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, at a dignified pace.
- Passengers booked to a place that empties while they're aboard still expect to be let off there. They'll be disappointed.
- A grown site doesn't look bigger from outside, only from inside. The architects are working on it.
