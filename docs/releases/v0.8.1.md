Far Haul 0.8.1 for Windows, in which the ship starts to show its age, and you get to do something about it with a wrench.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.8.1.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they contain exactly zero spares. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load, with every part as good as new. Enjoy it while it lasts.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

### Wear and tear
- The ship's working parts wear out: engines, radiators, braking and manoeuvring thrusters, and lander legs.
- Engines wear as they burn, faster when the ship's running hot. Radiators wear when they're working hard, and much faster if you let the ship overheat. Thrusters wear as they fire. Legs wear on every landing.
- Knocks to the hull shake things loose, so the station you bounced off costs more than the dent.
- Autopilot trips wear the engines too. The autopilot is careful, not magic.
- A worn part works less well: a tired engine pushes less, a tired radiator sheds less heat. A failed part does nothing at all. Lose every engine and port control won't let you leave, which is fair.

### Knowing about it
- At 25% condition the cockpit tells you a part's worn. When one fails, the alarm goes and the cockpit tells you in red.
- The HUD lists the parts in trouble, and so does the ship remote.
- The repair desk shows the state of every working part.

### Fixing it yourself
- On a space walk, worn parts are jobs, tagged with their name and condition so you can find them.
- Take the **wrench** (**1**) and hold **E** to service a worn part. It's free, takes six seconds, and gets it back up to 50%. That's as far as a wrench goes.
- A failed part needs a **spares kit** (1,500 cr at the repair desk, four to a locker). Fit it with the wrench and the part works again, at 40%.

### Paying someone else
- The repair desk will **overhaul the working parts**: everything back to new, priced by how worn it all is.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, wheezing slightly.
- One generic part for each job, so no manufacturers with better or worse parts yet.
- Parts don't show their wear from outside, apart from failing to do anything. The name tags on a space walk are your clue.
