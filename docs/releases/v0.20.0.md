Far Haul 0.20.0 for Windows, in which people start keeping score, and comparing notes.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.20.0.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they've never hauled anything. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

### Who remembers you
- **Every place** keeps its own opinion: the port you deliver to on time, the camp whose crates you broke, the downport where you cancelled on someone's grandmother.
- **Every species** has a view. Rescue a Kesh crew and the Kesh hear about it. All of them, eventually.
- **The authorities** keep a record: docking without clearance, landing unannounced, flying past a mayday. Rescues and reported wrecks go on it too, on the good side.
- What a place thinks of you is all three added up. The freight office will tell you, and show you who thinks what if you ask.

### Word travels
- Your own system knows at once. Other systems hear when the news gets there, as fast as the traffic carrying it. You can outrun a bad reputation for a few days. Not forever.

### What it gets you
- **Respected**: the best pay (a quarter over the usual), the lowest fees, and private contracts from people who only deal with captains everyone speaks well of.
- **Trusted**: priority berths. Traffic control stops making you wait for the other fellow.
- **Known**: sealed crates, as before, now per place.
- Traffic control says what it thinks of you, out loud, in every species' voice.

### What it costs you
- **Doubtful**: fewer offers, lower pay, higher fees. People look at you a moment longer.
- **Unwelcome**: board freight only. Nobody asks you for anything in person, and crew won't sign on.
- **Barred**: no work, and traffic control refuses you clearance. Dock anyway and it's a fine and another mark. If you're stranded, the tug still comes, with a lecture and a bill.
- Crew check what their own people say about you before they sign.

### The way back
- Settle your record with a system's authorities at the port's freight office. It costs what it costs.
- Take the work a place will still give you.
- Bad marks fade, about a tenth a week. Good ones don't. That's not fair, but neither is freight.

### Also
- Broken cargo now costs standing with the consignee, not just money.
- Saves from before this version keep their standing, at the port they were saved at.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, at a dignified pace.
- One number per relationship. Nobody yet tells your punctuality apart from your paperwork.
