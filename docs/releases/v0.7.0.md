Far Haul 0.7.0 for Windows, in which you can finally slow down without turning your back on the problem, and go sideways on purpose.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.7.0.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they contain exactly zero thrusters. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

### Braking thrusters
- A new bolt-on part in the shipyard, next to the lander legs: a slim fairing that lies flat against the hull, with two small nozzles in its nose that face the bow and push the ship backward. 20 kN each, about a quarter of a main engine. 9,000 cr, 0.6 t.
- Optional. Fit them on any bare side of the hull: beside the cockpit, on top, underneath, or on the nose if you don't need to see out. It lies along whichever side it's bolted to. Fit more than one if you like stopping in a hurry.
- They only brake if the nozzles face forward. Bolt one on backwards and the builder will tell you, with the patience of someone who has seen it before.
- The stats panel says how hard they slow the loaded ship. On the starter, one pod takes about 0.7 m/s off every second.

### Using them
- Hold **B** in flight to fire them. They burn fuel like everything else, and a damaged hull, an overheated ship or a power shortfall weakens them the same way it weakens the engine.
- The braking autopilot (**X**) uses them too. Moving more or less nose first and slow enough to stop on them, it brakes nose-on instead of flipping tail-first, so a docking approach stays lined up. Too fast for them, and it flips and burns with the main engine as before.
- The HUD shows them working, and you can see them light up from outside.

### Manoeuvring thrusters
- Another bolt-on: a low puck that sits flat on the hull, with small nozzles round its rim and on its face. 10 kN each way a pod, 7,000 cr, 0.4 t. Fit a couple on different sides so there's a nozzle pointing every way you want to go.
- Hold **Ctrl** with the arrow keys to slide left, right, up or down without turning. The arrows on their own still pitch and yaw. Useful for lining up on a docking collar, or for getting out of the way of a station that was there first.
- Fit them square to the ship. Turned a quarter round, the side nozzles push fore and aft, and the builder will mention it.
- With braking thrusters fitted too, the braking autopilot uses them to take out sideways and vertical drift while it stops you, so it ends up stopped and lined up rather than just stopped.

### Esc is the pause menu. Everywhere.
- It used to mean "pause" in port, "go home" at the helm and "leave" in the shipyard. Captains who pressed it to put the kettle on were towed back to the dock, which is efficient but not what they meant. Now **Esc** pauses everywhere: at the helm, on foot aboard, in the suit, in the shipyard and at the terminal. Press it again to carry on.
- What it used to do has moved:
  - **T** at the helm is for calling for help: traffic control brings you in on the approach, the tug comes when you're out of fuel or the hull is breached, and on a moon it's autoland and auto lift-off.
  - **Turning back** on a run, **turning back before a jump** and **ending a practice flight** are buttons in the pause menu, along with the tug when it would come. Harder to do by accident, which is the point.
  - **L** leaves the shipyard (it saves on the way out, as before).
- Esc still closes the map first, and a desk in port, before it pauses anything.

### Also
- Short messages from the cockpit now show in free flight around the station, not only on a run. Previously they were said to an empty room.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, wheezing slightly.
- The ship remote has no B button. Its brake button runs the autopilot, which uses the thrusters when it can.
- Neither kind of thruster steers. Turning is still the arrows' job.
- The ship remote has no slide buttons either.
