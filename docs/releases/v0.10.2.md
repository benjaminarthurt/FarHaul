Far Haul 0.10.2 for Windows, in which the dead finally share what they were carrying.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.10.2.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they've already been picked clean. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

### Cracking the hold
- Every derelict now has a cargo hatch, outlined in yellow with a bar across it. Take the **torch** (**2**) and hold **E**. It takes longer than a panel. They didn't want anyone in there either.
- Once it's open, whatever they were carrying drifts out of the hold and tumbles there, waiting for somebody with no scruples and a tether.
- Hold **E** on something to take it. Any tool will do. One thing at a time, back to your own hatch.
- A wreck stranded out on a route by a jump that didn't finish carries a little more. Its jump drive is in there too. It's what failed.

### What's in there
- **Cargo crates**: a tonne or two of something somebody was being paid to deliver. It goes in your hold, so it needs room, and it makes the ship heavier.
- **Supplies**: patch kits and spares kits, straight into the lockers. If the lockers are full, the rest goes back out of the hatch.
- **Ship parts**: engines, radiators, tanks, thrusters, legs, the odd jump drive. They're bulky and they take cargo space too. No room in the hold? The suit tells you before you've hauled it all the way back.

### Selling it
- **Cargo** sells at the freight office to whoever at the port deals in those goods, at their going rate, less a little for the lack of paperwork. If nobody here wants it, the scrap dealer will take it, for less.
- **Parts** sell at the shipyard desk, priced on what they'd cost new and what state they're in. A failed jump drive is worth what you'd expect.
- The phone's Repairs tab sells both too.
- Salvage and contract freight are kept apart, so you'll never sell somebody's delivery by accident. On purpose is a different update.

### Fixed
- Wrecks stranded out on routes no longer turn up on runs down to a moon or a landing site, and the sensors wait their turn rather than talking over the cockpit.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, wheezing slightly.
- Salvaged parts can only be sold, not bolted onto your own ship. Yet.
- You still can't go inside a derelict. The hold comes to you.
