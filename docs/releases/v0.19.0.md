Far Haul 0.19.0 for Windows, in which the planets stop being scenery and start being somewhere to land, if you're careful.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.19.0.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they've never been out of orbit. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

### Downports
- Every system's main world now has a downport on its surface, a few kilometres outside its city, and a rough landing field down the road. Sol has one on Mars as well. Fifteen worlds in all; the habitats stay habitats.
- The city sits on the skyline. Big ones look big. At night the windows come on and the sky over them glows, which is how you find them.
- The downport's terminal is a port like any other, with a window onto the planet. The field has a shed and a windsock.
- Some worlds' air you can breathe: step outside with the visor up and no tank to watch. Keshar's is for the Kesh only. Nobody breathes Mars.

### Getting down
- Runs to a planet start at the top of its air, fast and a long way out. Come in **belly first**: the air brakes you and the heat gauge stays out of the red. Nose first, you cook. Tilt the belly to steer; light the lift jets near the ground to stop.
- Or press T and the autopilot flies the whole thing, from the top of the air to the pad. It won't land you at a field, which has no beacon; it gets you close and hands back.
- The moon's lander legs can't hold a ship up at one g. **Heavy lander legs** can, three of them for a loaded starter. Heavier worlds need more, or a lighter load.
- A **heat shield** (tiles under the hull) buys margin on the way in. It's optional, the way seatbelts are.
- Coming down is cheap on fuel: the air does the braking. Going up is not. If the tanks can't make orbit, the ship won't try.

### Weather
- Each world has its own: calm, rain, snow, dust, gales. Wind pushes you about on the way down and costs landing fuel. Downport control tells you which way it's blowing.
- A solar storm overhead can whip a calm day into a gale.

### Fresh food
- The farms send up fresh produce from the downports and fields. It pays well for a day and a half, then starts to smell, and the pay goes with it.

### Also
- Downports and fields grow and shrink with their trade, like everywhere else.
- Downport control and the field foremen speak in their own species' words, as recorded voices.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, at a dignified pace.
- Each world is about 40 km of land round the downport. Past that, the planet is a rumour.
- No wings. The ship is the brick it looks like, and flies like one.
