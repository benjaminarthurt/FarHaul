Far Haul 0.3.0 for Windows, in which known space finally gets some scenery worth hauling past, and Earth stops looking like a blue moon.

**Download** `FarHaul-windows-0.3.0.zip` under Assets below. Not the Source code files: those are GitHub's, and they contain exactly zero planets. Unzip it anywhere and run `FarHaul\FarHaul.exe`. Nothing is installed. Saves go in a `Far Haul saves` folder next to the exe, and saves from earlier versions still load.

**On first run** Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC", because the exe is not code-signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. It's a freighter game. The universe is big; the download isn't.

### Sol looks like Sol
- Earth used to be the same speckled rock as every moon, painted blue. It now has its real coastlines (from NOAA's public-domain elevation data), shining oceans, drifting clouds, a thin blue edge of air and city lights on the night side. You'll recognise it. Everyone does.
- Luna shows the face you know, and Sol's moon base now always sits on it, grey, instead of whatever moon the dice picked.
- Mars is red, with polar caps. Nobody was surprised.

### You can fly to Saturn now (bring tanks)
- Sol has three new places to fly to: the **Venus cloud port**, the **Jovian works** and the **Saturn ring yards**. Each has its own station shape, its own world in the window and freight on the board.
- Saturn has rings, with a gap in them and the planet's shadow across them. Jupiter has its bands and its spot. Venus is cloud all the way down, as advertised.
- It's a long way. Saturn is 8 km/s from Earth's port and the starter's tank won't get you there. Two more fuel tanks will, with a load. Fuel at the Jovian works is the cheapest in Sol, which is the only good news about Jupiter.

### Every other system gets a world of its own
- Each system's world is drawn from what the canon says lives there, and none of them is Earth with a paint job: Keshar's jade shallow seas under a yellow haze, Ilos's escarpments under violet air, Veyara's island arcs, Orun's dust, Hesperus's golden farm belts, Foundry's lava cracks glowing in the dark, Roosevelt's snow, Tidemark's endless storms, and the rest.
- Inhabited worlds light up on their night sides. Lastlight has exactly one lit settlement. It's the brightest thing for a very long way.
- Most systems now have a neighbour hanging in the distance too, usually the local gas giant that sells you fuel.
- Carver, Port Meridian and Waystation have nowhere nice to live, and the view is honest about it: a fuel giant, a ringed giant with an icy moon, and the bare rock Waystation is bolted to.

### Arriving now looks like arriving
- Fly a run to the moon station or the Saturn yards and the station and sky at the far end are theirs, not the ones you left. Same after a star jump. Previously every destination looked suspiciously like home.

### The hull is actually sealed
- Where the airlock meets the main hull, the rounded edges didn't quite meet, leaving a gap you could see into. They now close properly at every inside corner. A test now fires rays out from inside the hull in every direction and fails if any get out.

### The game found its voice
- Hints, prompts, warnings, the shipyard, the people at the bar and the info site now talk like a tired freight captain. Keys, prices and numbers are unchanged; only the attitude is new. The key strips at the bottom of the screen are still plain, because some things shouldn't be funny.

### Known limits
- 64-bit Windows (x86_64). ARM Surface devices run it through Windows' built-in emulation, wheezing slightly.
- Prototype numbers: prices, pay and tuning will change, usually at the worst possible moment.
- Mercury, Uranus and Neptune aren't on the map yet. They know what they did.
- No space walks, planets with air or crew yet. The view has improved. The coffee hasn't.
