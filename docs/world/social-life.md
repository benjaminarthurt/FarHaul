# Social Systems and Leisure

Education, healthcare, housing, welfare and retirement systems are part of the economy because they employ people, consume freight and influence migration. They are not assumed identical across jurisdictions, but every large population needs functional equivalents.

Media operates through real communications latency. Local news can be immediate while an outer-system market report reaches another star later. Information can therefore be stale without anyone behaving irrationally.

Leisure matters because ordinary people do things besides work. Sports, racing, music, outdoor recreation, games and hobbies create venues, passenger movement, hotel demand and event freight while making settlements feel inhabited.
